📖 The production playbook

The hybrid architecture

Real autonomous vehicle systems use a hybrid approach:

  • Frozen base model — trained on billions of examples, never modified in the field
  • Per-environment LoRA adapters — lightweight (1-5% params), switchable: highway, urban, snow, night
  • Experience memory — stores recent encounters for periodic replay (consolidation)
  • EWC regularization — safety net that prevents catastrophic drift during any fine-tuning

The consolidation cycle

Experiences flow through a pipeline: raw events (dashcam frames) → vector store (embeddings) → periodic replay (nightly training) → adapter update. This is the "sleep" cycle of the system — it consolidates new knowledge without disrupting service.

Cost vs. safety

Every training run costs compute. But a car that forgets how to handle rain is dangerous. The engineering challenge is finding the cheapest approach that keeps the worst-case accuracy above a safety threshold. That's what this dashboard helps you explore.

Key insight

There's no single best method. The right answer is always a combination, tuned to your specific constraints: how much memory you can afford, how different the tasks are, and what your safety threshold is. That's the art of continual learning engineering.
