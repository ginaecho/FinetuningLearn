Case 07 · Capstone: Streaming Self-Driving Updates

Cost vs accuracy vs forgetting: build the hybrid memory system that beats them all.

1 · The hybrid architecture — how production systems actually work

Real-time path (cheap): every new experience goes into the vector DB and can be retrieved immediately. Slow path (consolidation): periodically, a replay batch from the DB updates the LoRA, turning recurring patterns into compressed weights.
📖 Why a hybrid beats pure replay or pure retrieval?

The three layers

  • Foundation model — frozen general knowledge (language, physics, road rules).
  • Stable LoRA — core driving skills learned via periodic replay. Updated monthly/quarterly. Fast at inference.
  • Experience memory — vector DB of raw episodes. Updated in real time. Retrieved for rare or novel situations.

Why not just retrieval?

Retrieval has no generalization. If the DB is down, the knowledge is gone. And you can't fit 100M experiences into a context window.

Why not just replay?

Retraining is expensive and slow. You don't want to re-certify a safety-critical model every day.

The balance

Retrieval handles day-to-day surprises (construction zone, local quirks). Replay consolidates recurring patterns into weights quarterly. This mirrors human cognition: hippocampus (fast, episodic) → cortex (slow, generalized).

Analogy

Replay = studying: a student practices hundreds of problems until concepts become automatic. They no longer need the textbook.

📖 How do I balance cost, accuracy, and forgetting?

The three axes

  • Cost — GPU hours spent on retraining. Naive is cheap per step but needs many steps. Retrieval is free.
  • Accuracy — average performance across all tasks seen so far.
  • Forgetting — how much old-task performance dropped. Retrieval has zero forgetting; naive has lots.

What does the replay frequency slider do?

In the hybrid system, you choose how often to run consolidation:

  • Low frequency (rare replay) → cheap, but weights lag behind new patterns. Accuracy suffers.
  • High frequency (frequent replay) → accurate, but GPU bill rises.
  • The sweet spot depends on your data velocity and budget. Automotive systems often choose quarterly.

Why the hybrid wins

Retrieval gives you immediate access to every experience (no forgetting, fast updates). Periodic replay compresses recurring experiences into weights (generalization, no retrieval latency). Together they cover both fast-changing specifics and stable core skills.
